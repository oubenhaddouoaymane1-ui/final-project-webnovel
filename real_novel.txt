Chapter One: The Crimson Tower

The rain had not stopped for seven days.

Kael stood at the edge of the Blackwood Bridge, his cloak heavy with water, and stared at the Crimson Tower rising from the mist like a wound in the sky. Its walls were ancient, built three centuries ago by the sorcerer-kings of Yrath, and they glowed faintly even in daylight — a deep arterial red that pulsed as if the stone itself had a heartbeat.

"Are you certain about this?" asked Sera, pulling her hood tighter against the downpour. Her emerald eyes reflected the tower's glow. She was shorter than Kael by a head, but carried herself like someone twice her size — straight-backed, chin raised, one hand resting on the hilt of her curved blade.

Kael did not turn. "The girl is in there. That is all I need to know."

"The girl is a prisoner of the Ashen Council. They have a hundred soldiers between us and her cell. You have a sword and a death wish."

"I have you," he said quietly.

Sera said nothing for a long moment. The river beneath them roared, swollen with rain, carrying fallen branches and debris toward the distant sea. On the far bank, the tower's reflection shimmered in the churning water like a bleeding wound.

"Then we go through the servant's gate," she finally said, producing a crumpled map from her coat. "Maren's intelligence says there is a passage beneath the kitchens. It was sealed after the last revolt, but the locks are iron, and I brought the right tools."

Kael turned to look at her for the first time. His face was angular, weathered by years of travel, with a scar running from his left temple to his jaw — a souvenir from the Battle of Hollowfield where he had lost an entire company of soldiers. His dark hair was cropped short, practical for a fighter, and his eyes were the color of storm clouds.

"How long do we have before dawn?"

"Four hours. The night shift changes at the fourth bell, and there is a gap — thirty seconds, maybe less — when the eastern corridor is empty."

"Then we move now."

They crossed the bridge in silence, boots splashing through puddles that reflected the crimson light above. The Blackwood Forest pressed in on both sides, ancient oaks twisted into impossible shapes by decades of exposure to the tower's ambient magic. Some of the trees had faces — not carved, but grown into the bark by some forgotten enchantment. Their wooden eyes followed the two travelers as they passed.

At the forest's edge, a crumbling stone wall marked the boundary of the tower's grounds. Beyond it, the courtyard stretched barren and grey, dotted with guard towers and the black iron cages where the Council displayed its prisoners. Most were empty tonight, swaying in the wind like gallows without bodies. But in the tallest cage, suspended thirty feet above the ground, Kael could see a small figure curled against the rain.

Evena.

She was only twelve years old, but the Ashen Council had accused her of sorcery — of channeling the old magic that had been forbidden since the fall of Yrath. If found guilty, she would be burned at the stake during the Equinox Festival, three days from now. The Council wanted to make an example of her. They wanted the people of the borderlands to see what happened to those who dared use magic outside their control.

"She is alive," Kael said, his voice tight.

"I can see that. What I cannot see is how we get her down without being noticed."

"The bell tower." Kael pointed to the narrow spire at the compound's northwest corner. "If we reach the top, we can use the rope mechanism they use to raise and lower the Council's banner. It is strong enough to support her weight."

"And the guards?"

"I will handle the guards."

Sera raised an eyebrow. "All forty of them?"

"Not all at once."

They slipped through a gap in the wall where the mortar had crumbled away, dropping into a drainage ditch that ran along the compound's inner perimeter. The ditch was knee-deep in muck and smelled of rot, but it provided cover from the guard towers. They moved quickly, staying low, following the ditch as it curved toward the kitchens.

The servant's gate was exactly where Maren had said it would be — a small iron door set into the base of the kitchen wing, half-hidden behind a rain barrel. The lock was rusted but intact. Sera knelt beside it, producing a set of thin metal picks from her belt.

"How did you learn to do that?" Kael asked, watching her work.

"My mother was a thief before she was a seamstress. She said the two skills had more in common than people realized."

The lock clicked open. Sera smiled in the darkness and pushed the door inward.

The corridor beyond was narrow and pitch-black. Kael lit a small phosphorescent stone — one of the few magical items he still carried — and its pale blue glow revealed walls of damp stone and a ceiling so low he had to duck. They moved quickly through the passage, their footsteps echoing softly.

After two turns, the corridor opened into a larger space — the tower's main stairwell, spiraling upward into darkness. From somewhere above came the muffled sound of voices and the clink of armored soldiers.

"The bell tower is three floors up and to the left," Sera whispered. "The eastern corridor should be clear in —" she checked a small brass chronometer on her wrist "— twelve minutes."

They waited in the shadow of the stairwell, listening. Above them, guards shuffled and muttered. One of them coughed. Another laughed at something, the sound sharp and cruel. These were the Council's elite — the Ashen Guard, named for the grey uniforms they wore and the ash they rubbed into their faces as war paint.

"Sera," Kael said softly. "When we reach Evena, I need you to get her out. Take her through the underground passage and back to the bridge. I will meet you there."

"And what will you be doing?"

"Something I should have done a long time ago."

Sera looked at him. In the blue glow of the phosphorescent stone, his scarred face looked like something carved from granite — hard, unyielding, and very old. She had traveled with him for three years now, across seven kingdoms and through countless dangers, and she had never once seen him show fear. She was not sure if that was courage or something else entirely.

"You are not going to come back, are you?" she asked.

Kael did not answer. The fourth bell began to toll, its deep resonance vibrating through the stone walls. When it faded, the corridor above fell silent.

"Now," he said.

They moved.

The eastern corridor was empty, just as Maren had promised. They crossed it in seconds, reaching the base of the bell tower. The door was unlocked — the bell-ringer, a old man named Osmund, had been dismissed from his duties weeks ago after refusing to ring the death knell for a political prisoner. The Council had not yet replaced him.

The bell tower's interior was cramped, filled with the massive rope mechanism and the clockwork gears that controlled the bell's movement. Kael began to climb the ladder built into the wall, Sera close behind. The rungs were slick with moisture, and more than once Kael felt his grip slide. But he did not stop.

At the top, a narrow platform offered a view of the entire compound. The rain was beginning to ease, and through the breaking clouds, Kael could see the crimson tower in all its terrible beauty — its walls pulsing, its windows like burning eyes. And suspended in the cage above the courtyard, Evena, her small face pale and streaked with dirt, her hands bound with iron chains that抑制 her magic.

Kael felt something tighten in his chest. He had failed to protect many people in his life. He would not fail this one.

"Sera. The banner rope. Can you reach it?"

She looked at the heavy braided cord that ran from the bell mechanism to the banner pole extending over the courtyard. It was ten feet away, across a gap that would require jumping.

"I can reach it," she said, gauging the distance.

"Do it. I will create a distraction."

"What kind of —"

But Kael was already descending, his face set like stone. He moved with a speed that seemed impossible for a man his age, dropping down the ladder and into the corridor. Sera heard his footsteps recede, then the distant sound of shouting.

She did not hesitate. She ran to the edge of the platform, coiled her legs, and jumped.

The rope caught under her fingers, and she swung outward over the courtyard, rain lashing her face. Below, soldiers were running toward the main stairwell — toward whatever Kael had done. She could hear the alarm bells beginning to ring, their pitch discordant and panicked.

She climbed hand over hand, moving toward the cage. The wind picked up, threatening to throw her off the rope, but she held on with the stubbornness of someone who had survived worse. When she reached the cage, she drew her blade and began cutting through the chains.

"Who are you?" Evena whispered, her eyes wide with terror and wonder.

"A friend. Hold on."

The chains fell away. Sera pulled the girl close and began her descent back toward the bell tower. Behind them, the courtyard erupted in fire.

Kael had found the powder stores.

The explosion lit up the night sky, a column of orange and red that rivaled the Crimson Tower's glow. The eastern wall of the compound collapsed inward, crushing guard towers and scattering soldiers like leaves in a storm. And in the chaos, Sera carried Evena to safety, running through the servant's gate and across the Blackwood Bridge while the tower burned behind them.

She did not look back. She knew what Kael had done. She knew he would not be following.

By the time she reached the forest's edge, the rain had stopped for the first time in seven days. The clouds parted, revealing a sky full of stars. Evena clung to her, trembling, and Sera held her tight and whispered that it was over, that she was safe, that the magic inside her was not a curse but a gift.

Behind them, the Crimson Tower's glow began to fade. The pulse in the stone slowed, and then stopped, as if the ancient structure was finally at peace.

And somewhere in the darkness, a man with a scarred face and storm-grey eyes watched from the shadows, making sure they made it home.
